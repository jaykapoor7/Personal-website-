The Prevention Paradox

How do you measure the outbreak that never happened?

Open experiment 05
FIG 05
QuestionDataModelLimitsIn practiceSources

The question

A quiet year after a campaign tells you what happened under the campaign. What would have happened without it is the counterfactual. CDC distinguishes tracking outcomes from attributing them to a program. Hernán and Robins introduce causal effects through comparisons of potential outcomes.

From the StrayPaw register

We use the count of 424 animals in the Mohanpur census card as the modeled community size, and set the campaign to reach half (212 animals). The card provides a register count, not a verified population estimate. The 50% campaign, introductions, timing and outbreak outcomes are hypothetical. This is not a reconstruction of a Mohanpur outbreak.

Snapshot 2026-11-10 · Source record or catalogue

How the model works

Each closed, well-mixed community contains 424 individuals, with the same three infections introduced into both. The campaign gives 212 other individuals complete immunity before introduction. The infectious period is one weekly step. The transmission coefficient 1.85 is illustrative. The chart stops after 18 weeks.

Weekly infection probability

pₜ = 1 − exp(−1.85 Iₜ / N)

Iₜ is the number infectious this week and N is the modeled community size. Each susceptible individual has probability pₜ of becoming infectious next week. The currently infectious group then recovers and is immune.
Paired modeled difference

Δ = C₁₈(no campaign) − C₁₈(campaign)

C is cumulative infection, including the three imported infections. Both worlds share indexed random draws for each individual and week.

How far the result goes

One pair shows one possible difference. The comparison is conditional on the simplified disease and population model, and chance can produce a small outbreak even without the campaign. This is a generic teaching epidemic with recovery. It provides no estimate of the effect of any real campaign.

What to measure in practice

Design evaluation alongside the rollout. A credible comparison might use randomized rollout or comparable areas, depending on feasibility, the design’s assumptions and the possibility of spillovers.
