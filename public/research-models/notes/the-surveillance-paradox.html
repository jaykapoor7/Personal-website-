Note 02 / Disease surveillance

The Surveillance Paradox

What if more reported cases means the system is getting better?

Open experiment 02
FIG 02
QuestionDataModelLimitsIn practiceSources

The question

When incident reports rise, can you distinguish more incidents from better detection? CDC’s surveillance manual emphasizes that interpretation requires knowing how the reporting system works and what changed. Here we know the true count because we created it.

From the StrayPaw register

StrayPaw’s historical field register includes 60 animal-care cases classified as Dog bite in 2024 and 34 in 2025. These are animal-care records, so they cannot estimate human bite incidence or all bites in a city. To give the experiment a register-scale starting point, divide the 24-month mean by the scenario’s assumed average reporting probability of 47.5%, then round. The resulting 8 latent cases per month are simulated. We do not estimate real reporting completeness from this register.

Snapshot 2026-11-10 · Source record or catalogue

How the model works

The simulation holds actual bite-related animal-care cases at 8 per month. Reporting starts at 15% in January and changes linearly to the slider’s December value. An independent random draw decides whether each incident is recorded. The same draws are reused as the slider moves.

Observation model

Yₘ | Nₘ, pₘ ∼ Binomial(Nₘ, pₘ)

Each of the Nₘ simulated incidents in month m has an independent chance pₘ of reaching the register.
Expected reports

E[Yₘ] = Nₘ pₘ

A rise in either the true count or detection probability can increase the expected number of reports.

How far the result goes

No false reports, duplicates, delayed entries, changing population or severity differences are simulated. Actual dog-bite reports can mix all of those effects. The hidden series is known only inside this simulation.

What to measure in practice

Record intake-channel launches and changes in reporting coverage alongside counts. Where possible, compare routine reports with independent surveys or audits and track reporting delays.
