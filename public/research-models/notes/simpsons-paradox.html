Note 03 / Aggregation & causal inference

Simpson’s Paradox

How can both groups improve while the overall result gets worse?

Open experiment 03
FIG 03
QuestionDataModelLimitsIn practiceSources

The question

Can an overall follow-up rate fall while rates for both types of cases rise? Yes. The categories in this example have different starting rates and their shares shift over time. Hernán and colleagues show why the causal meaning of such a reversal depends on the process that produced the data.

From the StrayPaw register

We selected two well-represented categories whose mix shifted between two complete calendar years. The denominator includes only road accidents and TVT (transmissible venereal tumour). Case counts are observed in StrayPaw. Completed follow-ups and their rates are constructed to demonstrate the reversal. The choice of the two categories is part of the example.

Snapshot 2026-11-10 · Source record or catalogue

How the model works

Road-accident records number 278 in 2024 and 133 in 2025. TVT records number 105 and 83. Apply illustrative completion probabilities of 90% and 92% to road accidents and 40% and 42% to TVT. Round the completed counts to whole cases before aggregating. The overall simulated rates are 292/383 and 157/216.

Overall rate

Rₜ = Σg completed(g,t) / Σg cases(g,t)

A weighted average of the two case types. Each weight is the type’s share of the combined number of cases that year.
Changing weights

TVT share = 105 / 383 → 83 / 216

TVT gets more weight in 2025, even as its illustrative follow-up rate improves.

How far the result goes

Case type is a descriptive grouping here. For a causal question, stratifying on a variable can reduce confounding or introduce bias, depending on the causal structure. Rates from a few cases can also vary substantially by chance. This example is arithmetic and makes no claim about sampling precision.

What to measure in practice

Keep numerator and denominator visible in aggregate and stratified reports. If comparing performance over time, show changing case mix and consider a rate standardized to an explicitly chosen common mix.
