Note 04 / Population dynamics

The Sterilization Paradox

How can thousands of surgeries produce almost no immediate decline?

Open experiment 04
FIG 04
QuestionDataModelLimitsIn practiceSources

The question

Why can surgery totals rise much faster than a city’s dog count falls? The experiment tracks dogs through maturation, births, deaths and immigration. A published model by Shamsaddini and colleagues studied how female neutering could affect free-roaming dog numbers under local demographic assumptions in southeastern Iran. This model uses its own illustrative parameters.

From the StrayPaw register

The initial slider uses the median of 42 months with at least one linked female sterilization entry in the 2013–2016 HSI archive. It gives the model a historically grounded surgery cadence. The archive is a clinical register. It does not establish true program capacity, the city’s starting dog population, or the number that remained after treatment. The population trajectory, baseline and demographic parameters are modeled.

Snapshot 2026-11-10 · Source record or catalogue

How the model works

The initial 10,000 dogs include 3,600 fertile adult females, 400 sterilized adult females, 4,000 adult males and 2,000 juveniles. Surgery precedes births. Each month 1.2% of existing adults and 7.5% of existing juveniles die. One sixth of surviving juveniles mature. There are 105 adult arrivals per month. Maturing and arriving dogs are split evenly between fertile females and males. The baseline uses the same rules at zero additional surgeries.

Monthly population balance

Nₜ₊₁ = Nₜ + birthsₜ + arrivalsₜ − deathsₜ

Surgery moves an adult female between compartments. Maturation moves a juvenile to an adult compartment. Both transfers conserve total dogs.
Births after surgery

Bₜ = 0.16 (Fₜ − Sₜ) max(0, 1 − Nₜ / 18,000)

F is fertile adult females and S is the smaller of capacity and the number available. Density is computed at the start of the month.

How far the result goes

Counts are deterministic expectations, so fractional dogs can appear in the calculations. Labels round for display. Births depend on fertile females and density. The model omits seasonality, age-specific adult mortality, spatial structure and emigration, and holds immigration and mortality rates fixed.

What to measure in practice

Compare procedure records with repeat population estimates and indicators such as juvenile share, sterilization coverage and incoming dogs. A surgery ledger answers a different question from a population survey.
