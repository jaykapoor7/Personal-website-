Experiment 03 / Aggregation & causal inference

How can both groups improve while the overall result gets worse?

Separate the overall follow-up rate by case type to see what changed inside the average.

Simpson’s ParadoxSimulation · StrayPaw data
What this shows

Both simulated follow-up rates rose. Cases with the lower rate made up a bigger share of the total.

From the StrayPaw register

27% → 38% TVT share of the two selected case types in StrayPaw’s historical register, 2024 to 2025.

How we use this data
Assumptions and methodology

Road-accident records number 278 in 2024 and 133 in 2025. TVT records number 105 and 83. Apply illustrative completion probabilities of 90% and 92% to road accidents and 40% and 42% to TVT. Round the completed counts to whole cases before aggregating. The overall simulated rates are 292/383 and 157/216.

Overall rate

Rₜ = Σg completed(g,t) / Σg cases(g,t)

A weighted average of the two case types. Each weight is the type’s share of the combined number of cases that year.
Changing weights

TVT share = 105 / 383 → 83 / 216

TVT gets more weight in 2025, even as its illustrative follow-up rate improves.

Case type is a descriptive grouping here. For a causal question, stratifying on a variable can reduce confounding or introduce bias, depending on the causal structure. Rates from a few cases can also vary substantially by chance. This example is arithmetic and makes no claim about sampling precision.
