Experiment 02 / Disease surveillance

What if more reported cases means the system is getting better?

Move the slider to improve reporting. Then reveal how many cases actually occurred in the simulation.

The Surveillance ParadoxSimulation · StrayPaw data
What this shows

More reports can reflect better detection. The reports alone cannot tell you what happened to the true rate.

From the StrayPaw register

94 historical animal-care records classified as Dog bite across 2024 and 2025.

How we use this data
Assumptions and methodology

The simulation holds actual bite-related animal-care cases at 8 per month. Reporting starts at 15% in January and changes linearly to the slider’s December value. An independent random draw decides whether each incident is recorded. The same draws are reused as the slider moves.

Observation model

Yₘ | Nₘ, pₘ ∼ Binomial(Nₘ, pₘ)

Each of the Nₘ simulated incidents in month m has an independent chance pₘ of reaching the register.
Expected reports

E[Yₘ] = Nₘ pₘ

A rise in either the true count or detection probability can increase the expected number of reports.

No false reports, duplicates, delayed entries, changing population or severity differences are simulated. Actual dog-bite reports can mix all of those effects. The hidden series is known only inside this simulation.
