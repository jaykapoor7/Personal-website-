Experiment 04 / Population dynamics

How can thousands of surgeries produce almost no immediate decline?

Slide to change monthly female sterilizations. Watch the first year and the years that follow.

The Sterilization ParadoxSimulation · StrayPaw data
What this shows

Sterilization changes future births. Survival, aging and incoming dogs still shape the population you see today.

From the StrayPaw register

278 median recorded female sterilizations per active month in the Jamshedpur archive.

How we use this data
Assumptions and methodology

The initial 10,000 dogs include 3,600 fertile adult females, 400 sterilized adult females, 4,000 adult males and 2,000 juveniles. Surgery precedes births. Each month 1.2% of existing adults and 7.5% of existing juveniles die. One sixth of surviving juveniles mature. There are 105 adult arrivals per month. Maturing and arriving dogs are split evenly between fertile females and males. The baseline uses the same rules at zero additional surgeries.

Monthly population balance

Nₜ₊₁ = Nₜ + birthsₜ + arrivalsₜ − deathsₜ

Surgery moves an adult female between compartments. Maturation moves a juvenile to an adult compartment. Both transfers conserve total dogs.
Births after surgery

Bₜ = 0.16 (Fₜ − Sₜ) max(0, 1 − Nₜ / 18,000)

F is fertile adult females and S is the smaller of capacity and the number available. Density is computed at the start of the month.

Counts are deterministic expectations, so fractional dogs can appear in the calculations. Labels round for display. Births depend on fertile females and density. The model omits seasonality, age-specific adult mortality, spatial structure and emigration, and holds immigration and mortality rates fixed.
